Core Owlplay Principles

    DRUBoomkin Kit (your teammate)›
    • Cyclone — full disable, no damage/healing while active. The single best pure lockdown on your team; use it on the biggest current threat, not just defensively.
    • Faerie Fire — 40s duration, reveals stealth/invis + shreds armor. Cast on any rogue on sight; it removes his ability to vanish-reset.
    • Moonfire / Insect Swarm — sustained DoT pressure; Insect Swarm also reduces enemy hit chance, useful chip value vs melee.
    • Starfire / Wrath — main direct damage, fully interruptible casts with no defensive cover while channeling.
    • Force of Nature — 2 treants that auto-root on arrival; free extra CC/peel on a cooldown, use proactively.
    • Entangling Roots / Nature's Grasp — standard lockdown plus a passive proc that roots melee attackers.
    • Barkskin — 20% less damage for 12s, the only defensive CD. No CC immunity like Ice Block — pop it under pressure, not to "tank" a stun/fear chain.
    • Innervate — hand this to the Resto Shaman in any long game; the comp's main mana-sustain tool.
    • Improved Moonkin Form — passive team-wide spell/melee crit aura while shapeshifted; shifting out (e.g. to clear a root) drops it until shifted back.
    • Hibernate — works on Beast/Dragonkin, and on enemy Druids in Cat/Bear Form or Shamans in Ghost Wolf. Situational bonus CC a Frost Mage never had.

    Balance Druid (Boomkin) / Shadow Priest / Resto Shaman, from your Draenei SPriest seat.

    Honest framing on this comp

    By current TBC meta consensus this is a real power downgrade from Shatterplay, not a lateral swap — Balance Druid isn't tier-listed as arena-viable at all, and you're trading a top burst/control caster for an off-meta CC-and-attrition hybrid with no interrupt and no Ice-Block-tier defensive. That said it's not pure downside: Faerie Fire directly solves your team's #1 flagged loss condition (rogues resetting into stealth), Cyclone is arguably the best single-target lockdown in the game, and Innervate gives your cast-reliant Resto Shaman a mana tool the mage never had. The matchups below are already showing real, logged Owlplay results — not just theorycraft — so trust the data as it accumulates.

    The 3 Phases of Every Game

    1
    Open — first 10-15s. No shatter combo to wait for anymore — instead your Boomkin is hunting for a clean Faerie Fire on any rogue and a Cyclone window on their priority target, while you apply MF/VT and hold your CC for the read, and your shaman watches for the opposing kill target. This is where most losses (yours and the old Shatterplay log's) actually happen — caught off guard, split up, your own flank trained.
    2
    Mid-game — no Ice Block or Cloak-style CD to bait anymore, and no interrupt beyond your own Silence. This phase is about grinding: land Cyclone on the biggest live threat while DoTs/Mind Flay chip away, and use Force of Nature/Roots to buy time rather than forcing one explosive window.
    3
    Close — CDs are down, someone is exposed. Full commit, chain your CC on the healer/off-target so they can't peel, finish in order. Unchanged from Shatterplay — this part doesn't depend on who your 3rd DPS is.
    Your teammate: the Boomkin's job›
    • No interrupt, no Ice Block. The Boomkin can't lock an enemy caster off a school or trinket-out of a CC chain the way a Frost Mage could — your Silence is now the team's only interrupt, so use it on the highest-value cast you can, not reactively on whatever's already resolving.
    • Faerie Fire is close to mandatory upkeep against any comp with a rogue or a druid — it denies stealth/vanish resets and shreds armor. A missing Faerie Fire on an enemy rogue is a live problem, not a minor omission.
    • Cyclone is the single strongest tool this comp brings — a full, un-resistible disable. It should usually go on the biggest live threat (a healer's CD window, a caster mid-setup, a target you need frozen for a swap) rather than being saved as a panic button.
    • Force of Nature (2 treants that auto-root) is free extra CC/peel on a cooldown — call for it proactively when a melee is about to close distance, not only after they've connected.
    • Innervate goes to the Resto Shaman by default in any game that runs long — this is the comp's main answer to mana attrition now that you can't reliably force a fast kill.
    • The Boomkin has meaningfully fewer defensive outs than a mage. Barkskin reduces damage, it doesn't grant CC immunity. Two of your own logged losses (vs RMP, vs Warlock/RShaman/RDruid) trace directly to the boomkin dying while the team played defensive — treat protecting him with the same urgency the old notes gave to protecting the shaman.
    Your job as Shadowpriest›
    • You have no personal defensive cooldown beyond Fade/Dispersion (later tier) and your trinket — this is exactly why you're a frequent kill target and why positioning beats reacting.
    • Your core toolkit: Mind Flay for pressure + silence-on-cast, Vampiric Touch/Shadow Word: Pain for sustained pressure that doesn't require you to stand and cast, Psychic Scream for a full-team fear (positioning-dependent, can save yourself or peel for your shaman), Dispel Magic for offensive dispel value (see class notes — Blessing of Sacrifice, Ice Barrier-type shields, HoTs).
    • Mind Flay's channel makes you vulnerable — don't commit to a full channel blind, especially if their kill target (you) hasn't been confirmed safe.
    • You're also the team's only interrupt now that there's no mage — Psychic Scream and Silence are position/timing tools as much as damage tools. Use them to buy your shaman and boomkin time, not only to secure kills.
    Talent & stat priorities (general TBC convention)›
    • Shadow Priest PvP builds conventionally prioritize Shadow Focus, Improved Shadow Word: Pain, Silence, and Psychic Horror/Vampiric Embrace as available — exact point allocation shifts by patch, so cross-check current talent calculators before finalizing.
    • Balance Druid leans into Moonkin Form, Improved Moonkin Form (team-wide crit aura), Insect Swarm, and enough Restoration points to reach Nature's Grasp / Cyclone access.
    • Resto Shaman runs deep Restoration for Nature's Swiftness, Earth Shield efficiency, and Tidal Focus/mana tools — this spec is more cast-reliant than other TBC healers, which is exactly why protecting your shaman early matters so much.
    • Resilience is your #1 PvP stat once you have your core damage/healing talents — it reduces incoming damage and CC duration on you.
    Addon setup›
    • Gladius (or Gladdy) — arena unit frames with CC/diminishing-return tracking. Non-negotiable for arena.
    • OmniCC / OmniBar — cooldown tracking for enemy trinkets, defensives, and CDs (Ice Block, Bestial Wrath, Death Wish, etc.) — referenced constantly in the how-to-kill breakdowns you already have.
    • A DR (diminishing returns) tracker — critical for knowing when your CC chain is actually going to land vs. resist/half-duration.
    Beginner mistakes (from your own loss data)›
    • Getting caught off guard in the opener. Multiple logged losses (across both eras of this comp) trace directly to this — slow down and confirm positioning before committing, especially against hunters and rogues.
    • Own shaman or boomkin getting isolated and killed first. The old mirror-match loss showed this with the shaman; two new Owlplay losses (vs RMP, vs Warlock/RShaman/RDruid) show the same pattern with the boomkin now that he has no Ice Block-tier escape. Either flank being pressured in the first 10s takes priority over your own damage.
    • Committing into a defensive CD instead of baiting it first. Fewer defensives to bait now (no Ice Block/Cloak-equivalent from your own team's 3rd), but this still applies to enemy defensives — don't dump Cyclone/CC into a cooldown, force it out first.
    • Not updating the kill target when the read changes. Wins consistently show target flips (rogue overextends → go rogue instead of the planned target); losses often show tunneling the original plan too long.

    • You're early on real Owlplay data (a handful of logged games vs. 35 from the Shatterplay era) — keep logging every game so the Plan tab's "you've logged this" branch has real reads instead of falling back to generated ones.
